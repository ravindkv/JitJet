\chapter{Preface: A Jet Is a Journey, Not a Destination}
\chaptermark{Preface}
\label{ch:preface}

In CMS an electron, a photon or a muon is a \emph{destination}: an object that
is essentially there at the end of reconstruction and needs only identification
and calibration. A jet is a \emph{journey}. It does not exist at any single
point; it is the accumulated history of a coloured parton from the hard
scattering to the calibrated, tagged, smeared four-vector an analyst finally
puts into a histogram. Every stage of that journey either adds energy to the
jet, removes energy from it, or reshuffles how the energy is distributed.

This book follows one and the same jet through every stage, shows the
mathematics of each stage, measures how much energy that stage added or
removed, and points to the official paper for further reading. The title is
meant literally: \emph{Journey Is The Jet}, and the two jets we follow are the
$b$-jet (bottom) and the top-jet (top).

\section*{How to read this book}

Chapters are stages of a pilgrimage told through one metaphor: a mother quark,
her children from the parton shower, the crowd on the road (pileup), borders and
checkpoints (detector and trigger), the census (Particle Flow), the police
(pileup mitigation), the inn (clustering), the tax office (jet energy
corrections), the wobbly ruler (resolution) and the registry office (tagging).
The metaphor is a memory aid; the physics is always stated explicitly.

\paragraph{The voice of this book.} Think of the journey as a guided tour.
The author is the guide and you are the passenger: at every stop the guide
explains what is happening outside the window, hands you the formula that
describes it, and asks you to press a button and see the number come out
yourself. The tone is deliberately that of a teacher standing beside you
rather than a reference manual in front of you. The guide's job is to keep
the passengers engaged, and a passenger who is reading a log file is not
looking out of the window. So the code that produces every number is not
printed in the book, and neither are the commands that run it, the logs it
writes or the configurations of the official packages. They live in the
public repository, in the three homes described next, so that the pages can
be spent on the physics.

\paragraph{The three homes of a stop.} Every chapter of the journey, every
stop, lives in three places:
\begin{description}[nosep]
  \item[\file{example/XYZ/}] the scripts, the event records, the logs, the
    plots and the films of the stop, one directory per stage.
  \item[\file{chapter/chapter_XYZ.tex}] the chapter you are reading: the
    physics, in the guide's voice.
  \item[\file{chapter/chapter_XYZ.md}] the technical companion of the
    chapter, a page on GitHub that is not compiled into the book: how to run
    each script and what it prints, how to read its records and logs, how
    each number of the official packages was obtained, and the full tables
    behind every number the chapter quotes.
\end{description}
A chapter prints exactly one address, that of its own companion. Everything
else, the repository, the scripts, the \CMSSW material, the films, is
reached through the \jitjetworld table at the end of this preface, and
chapters point at that table rather than repeating its addresses.

\paragraph{The shape of a stop.} Every chapter has the same shape, and the
order is deliberate: it runs from the top down, the way a guided tour does,
not from the bottom up, the way a textbook does. The chapter opens with
\textbf{the stage in the journey}, the metaphor in one paragraph, and
\textbf{the physics in this chapter}, one paragraph that ends with the one
number to take away from the stop. Then come four sections, always the same
four and always in this order.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{What we will see at this stop.} The motivation, connected to
    the previous stop; the film of the stage, rendered from the same code
    that produces the chapter's numbers, in one paragraph and one figure of
    key frames (the films are fully narrated, so the book does not retell
    them); a sketch of the stage, and a picture to carry if it helps; and the
    agreement table, one row per quantity, with the standalone script, the
    official package and \CMSSW side by side, explained in a paragraph or
    two. How the numbers were produced is in the companion, not here.
  \item \textbf{Let us dive into the details: the theory.} The minimal,
    correct mathematics of the stage. Every subsection is told the same way:
    a question to guess at, then a sketch or Feynman diagram, then the
    equation, then the numbers the equation gives for \emph{our} event,
    quoted in the text from the learning mode of the standalone script
    (never as a log listing), and then a plot of the equation over the whole
    phase space, one figure per equation, drawn from the same code. The
    theory ends with the recipe, step by step, that the script executes.
  \item \textbf{The energy ledger: our jet at this stage.} The ledger table
    and the $(\eta,\phi)$ display of our jet side by side, from the
    standalone script and from \CMSSW, cumulative since the first stop; then
    what this stop is, and what it is not.
  \item \textbf{Further reading.} The papers behind the stop, then
    \textbf{pitfalls and FAQs} for newcomers, \textbf{exercises}, and a list
    of \textbf{open problems} for the Phase-2 LHC with the state of the art
    and its references.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The companion.} Each companion has the same five sections, so
that you know where to look whatever the stop: running the standalone script
(the command, its options, what the learning modes \code{-v} and \code{-vv}
print); reading the record (the event record of the stage and the log
excerpts that the chapter quotes its numbers from); drawing the event and
rendering the film; validation from official packages (the \PYTHIA, \MG or
\FASTJET script, the \CMSSW configuration and log, and how each number was
read out of them); and the full tables, every number the chapter quotes with
the command that produced it.

\paragraph{The length of a stop.} A stop is $25$ pages, give or take five. A short
interlude, a stop with no standalone script of its own such as matching and
merging, is at most eight and has only the first section, the ``what it is
not'' and the further reading. Commands, logs and configurations are never
printed in the book; the only code typeset here is the TikZ of the sketches.
The budget is checked from the table of contents at every build.

\paragraph{Top to bottom, bottom to top.} The two phrases are not in
conflict. The \emph{journey} runs from the bottom up: it starts where the
jet starts, at the hard scattering, and ends where the jet ends, in the
histogram, and the two pilgrims are the bottom quark and the top quark.
Each \emph{stop} along the way is told from the top down: you see the stage
first, run it second, derive it third, and let the official packages have
the last word. Bottom to top is the road; top to bottom is how the guide
explains each stop.

\section*{The two pilgrims}

\begin{description}
  \item[Event A, the $b$-jet.] \code{generate p p > b b\~{}} at $\sqrt{s}=13.6\TeV$ in
    \MG with a fixed random seed, showered and hadronised with \PYTHIA (CP5 tune),
    premixed with Run~3 pileup, simulated with \GEANT, passed through Level-1 and
    High-Level Trigger emulation, reconstructed with Particle Flow, \PUPPI,
    \antikt $R=0.4$ clustering, jet energy corrections and resolution smearing,
    and finally $b$-tagged.
  \item[Event B, the top-jet.] \code{generate p p > t t\~{}} with a boosted
    hadronically decaying top quark ($\pt>400\GeV$), the same chain,
    \antikt $R=0.8$ clustering, soft drop, $N$-subjettiness and a
    machine-learned top tagger.
\end{description}

\section*{The energy ledger}

For both jets we keep an \emph{energy ledger}: one row per stage with the jet
\pt, mass, constituent multiplicity and the change with respect to the previous
stage. The ledger is the quantitative red thread of the book and is closed in
\cref{ch:energy_ledger}.
