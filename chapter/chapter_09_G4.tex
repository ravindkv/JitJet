\chapter{The Hand-over at the Border: \GEANT Primaries}
\chaptermark{Crossing the Border}
\label{ch:crossing_the_border}

\begin{journeybox}
The road ends at a gate. Behind it waits a foreign country, the CMS
detector of 2025, silicon, crystals, brass and iron in a field of
$3.8$~tesla, and nobody walks in without being looked at first. The guards
are not interested in luggage yet; they check papers. Who is too slow to
matter, who carries no passport the country recognises, who is walking
along the wall of the road and will never reach a town: they are turned
away before anyone has taken a step inside. A few are sent along a
different road, the rail line beside the beam, to two stations hundreds of
metres away. The family and the two crowds of the previous stop arrive
together, are sorted together, and gather on the other side of the gate,
ready to cross. The crossing is the next stop.
\end{journeybox}

\physicsline{the edges of the detector \GEANT is given, the beam pipe, the
hole of the forward calorimeter, the Zero Degree Calorimeter and the Roman
pots; the hand-over cuts of \CMSSW's \code{Generator.cc}: the very-forward
rule, the momentum cut and the pseudorapidity cut, which is a line from the
vertex and not an angle; the decays the generator has already made and the
cylinder of $2.9\cm$ that decides whether \GEANT gets the parent or the
daughters; the neutrinos killed before their first step; the forward
protons carried by the optics of the LHC to the Roman pots. The one number
to take away: \GEANT receives $90\,\%$ of the family's particles and $96\,\%$
of its transverse momentum, but only half of its energy, $52\,\%$ ($51\,\%$
in \CMSSW), and a quarter of the crowd's; the rest goes down the beam pipe
before the simulation has taken a single step.}

\section{What we will see at this stop}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:what_we_compute}

At the end of \cref{ch:crowd_on_the_road} the event was complete as a list
of particles: the family of \cref{ch:family_photograph}, $749$ stable
particles carrying the $13.6\TeV$ of the collision, and the crowd of its
bunch crossing, $63$ in-time collisions with $13\,456$ particles and $897$
collisions of the fifteen neighbouring crossings with $161\,696$, each
collision at its own vertex along the beam. Nothing of it has met matter
yet. From this stop on the particles are handed to \GEANT~\cite{GEANT4:2002zbu,Allison:2006ve,Allison:2016lfl},
the toolkit with which CMS, like every LHC experiment, simulates how
particles cross a detector, and the hand-over is the first thing that
happens: before \GEANT takes its first step, a piece of \CMSSW, the class
\code{Generator} of \file{SimG4Core/Generators/src/Generator.cc}, reads the
generator record and decides, particle by particle, which ones become
\GEANT \emph{primaries}. Its rules are few and written by hand in the
configuration of the simulation step, \file{g4SimHits_cfi.py}, and they
follow the edges of the detector: a cylinder of $2.9\cm$ around the beam, a
pseudorapidity of $5.5$, a momentum of $40\MeV$. Two more pieces decide who
enters: the \code{StackingAction}, which kills the neutrinos before their
first step, and the transport of the Precision Proton Spectrometer (PPS),
which carries the protons that survived the collision along the beam line
to the Roman pots, $203$\,m away.

This stop is therefore short, and it is a selection, not a
transformation: nothing moves, nothing loses energy, nothing is smeared.
Members of the family are dropped, and that is all. It deserves a stop of
its own for the reason every stop of the journey has one: energy leaves the
event here, and a great deal of it. The numbers to take away are four.
\GEANT receives $672$ of the family's $749$ particles, $90\,\%$ of them and
$96\,\%$ of their transverse momentum, but $7.14$ of its $13.6\TeV$, $52\,\%$:
the other half flies beyond $|\eta|=5.5$ with the remnants of the two
protons, where no detector can see it, and is dropped. The crowd loses
more: a quarter of its energy is handed over, $216\TeV$ of the $857\TeV$ of
the in-time collisions, while $124$ forward neutrons and photons are
handed over towards the Zero Degree Calorimeter and $27$ protons are
carried along the beam line to the Roman pots. The cut that does it is a line, not an angle: the
straight flight of a particle is followed from its own vertex to
$|z|=354.8\cm$, so that a collision $4\cm$ from the centre moves the
effective cut by $0.01$ in pseudorapidity (and the beam spot, a third of a
millimetre off the axis, by as much again), and in our window $114$
particles beyond $|\eta|=5.5$ are kept and $93$ inside it dropped. And our
jet crosses the gate whole, its $31$ particles and $99.83\GeV$ handed over
untouched, while in the \CMSSW event the $b$ jet loses its
antineutrino, $37.5\GeV$ of transverse momentum that every detector will
miss from here on: the missing transverse momentum of an event is decided
at this gate. As at the previous stops we meet the numbers from the top
down: a film shows the gate at work, a Python script needing only NumPy
writes out the decision of \code{Generator.cc} line by line on our record,
the theory explains each rule, and two official packages, the geometry
navigator of ROOT and the particle data of \PYTHIA, check it on exactly our
particles, with the \CMSSW job of the companion on its own event beside
them.

at a time, and the separation of the three crowds survives the gate intact,
until the digitisation of \cref{ch:customs_form} adds their signals.

\paragraph{The number, three ways.}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:validation}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:standalone_code}
\Cref{tab:crossing_the_border:numbers} gives away the ending. The standalone script of this stop is a port: the
loop of
\code{HepMC2G4}, the method of \code{Generator} that walks over the vertices of the event, its tests in the
order of the code, the line of the \code{StackingAction} that kills the
neutrinos and the selection of the PPS transport, applied to the record of
\cref{ch:crowd_on_the_road} and to the out-of-time collisions rebuilt from
its library. The sharpest check is again a comparison on identical input,
and here the official packages are two. The geometry is done by ROOT's
geometry package, TGeo, the navigator of the virtual Monte
Carlo~\cite{Brun:2003TGeo}: the two cylinders of \code{Generator.cc} are
built as TGeo tubes and every one of the $175\,901$ stable particles of the
window is started at its vertex and stepped to the boundary, and the
decisions agree for all but six, six particles of the out-of-time crowd
whose longitudinal momentum the library rounded to zero, which
\code{Generator.cc} literally keeps at their vertex and drops, and TGeo
lets out through the side of the tube. The generator's half of the decision,
which hadrons are already decayed when the hand-over starts, is checked
against the particle data of \PYTHIA~8.315 with the settings of
\CMSSW~\cite{Bierlich:2022pfr}: every species our record keeps stable is
one \PYTHIA leaves to \GEANT, every species it decayed one \PYTHIA decays.
The last column is the \CMSSW job of the companion, \code{CMSSW\_15\_0\_5}
with the global tag and the era of 2025, which hands the signal event and
the $1000$ collisions of its pileup library to \GEANT with the physics
reduced to the transportation, and counts what \GEANT received; its event
is another shower of the same hard event, and its crowd another draw, so
the comparison is of the rules and the proportions, not of the particles.

and the neutrinos, the forward neutrals and protons, the two $b$ systems and
our jet as \GEANT receives them, and the ledger; \code{-vv} adds every
particle of the family that does not become a primary and one line per
in-time collision. The \code{-v} log of seed~1 is committed, and every number
quoted in the theory of this chapter is taken from it. How each column of
the table was produced is in the companion of this chapter,

\section{Let us dive into the details: the theory}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:theory}

\subsection{The edges of the country: the detector \GEANT is given}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:detector}

The question first: why should anything be dropped at all? \GEANT could be
given every particle of the record and left to find out what the detector
sees. It is not, because the detector has edges, and beyond them a particle
costs simulation time and leaves nothing. The world of the simulation step
is the geometry of the global tag, the description of the 2024 detector
that the 2025 tag reads unchanged, a tree of volumes each with a shape, a
material and a place inside its mother~\cite{CMS:2008xjf,CMS:2024aqx}. For
the hand-over only its edges matter, \cref{fig:crossing_the_border:edges}:
where the material starts, the beam pipe, a beryllium tube whose central
part ends at $r=2.25\cm$; and where a particle must still be to be seen,
which in the forward direction is the forward calorimeter HF, between
$|z|=11.15$ and $12.80$\,m, with a hole for the beams of radius $12.5\cm$.

A straight line from the nominal collision point with pseudorapidity $\eta$
is at radius
\begin{equation}
  r(z)=\frac{|z|}{\sinh|\eta|},\qquad
  \eta=\sinh^{-1}\frac{|z|}{r},
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:line}
\end{equation}
so that an edge at $(r,z)$ is seen at $\eta=\sinh^{-1}(|z|/r)$. The corner of
the tracker volume, $(123.3, 293.2)\cm$, is at $|\eta|=1.60$; the hole of HF
begins to cost particles at $|\eta|=5.18$, the $\sinh^{-1}$ of
$1114.95/12.495$, where a line from the centre misses its front face, and
swallows everything beyond $|\eta|=5.32$, the $\sinh^{-1}$ of
$1280.05/12.495$, where the line passes through the whole hole. The
gate of the next subsection sits just beyond, at $5.5$. Further out the
detector is the beam line itself, the Zero Degree Calorimeter at $140$\,m and
the Roman pots at $203$\,m, \cref{sec:crossing_the_border:forward}. Between
the edges the figure shows what will matter from the next stop on: the
calorimeters sit inside the solenoid, the muon chambers outside it in the
steel that returns its field, and nothing but HF looks beyond $|\eta|=3.0$.

What the edges do to our event, from the \code{-v} log: the family's $b$
system leaves its vertex at $\eta=-1.38$, $0.22$ inside the corner of the
tracker, towards the end of the barrel calorimeters, and the $\bar b$ system at
$\eta=-3.43$, beyond the endcap calorimeters, into HF. But the edges matter
most for what is not ours. Beyond $|\eta|=5.5$ lie $67$ of the family's $749$
particles, $9\,\%$ of them, with $3\,\%$ of its transverse momentum,
$23\GeV$ of $743$, and $46\,\%$ of its energy, $6231$ of $13\,600\GeV$: the
remnants of the two protons. For the in-time crowd the proportions are
starker: $2599$ of $13\,456$ particles, and $764$ of its $857\TeV$, $89\,\%$
of the energy. \Cref{fig:crossing_the_border:plot_edges} shows the reason
on one page. The particles of a collision are spread almost evenly in
pseudorapidity out to $|\eta|\approx5$ and thin out beyond, panel (a); the
energy, $E=\pt\cosh\eta$, grows exponentially with $|\eta|$ and is piled up
where the particles are fewest, panel (b); and so the fraction of the
transverse momentum inside $|\eta|<5.5$ is $97\,\%$ for the family and
$85\,\%$ for the crowd, while the fraction of the energy is $54\,\%$ and
$11\,\%$, panel (c). The gate at $5.5$ keeps nearly every particle a
detector could measure and drops nearly all of the energy of the event.

\subsection{The eta cut is a line, not an angle}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:line}

The motivation for the next rule is that the edge of the previous
subsection is a place, not a direction. A particle is lost to the
detector if it stays inside the beam pipe until it is past the
calorimeters; whether that happens depends on where it starts as much as
on where it goes. \code{Generator.cc} therefore does not test the
pseudorapidity of the momentum. It takes the straight line of the particle
from its production vertex $(x_v,y_v,z_v)$, follows it along the beam to
$|z|=\Zmax$ on its own side, and asks whether it is still inside the
cylinder of radius $R$ (\code{RDecLenCut}) there,
\cref{fig:crossing_the_border:line}:
\begin{equation}
  \vec r_\perp(\pm\Zmax)=\vec r_{\perp,v}+(\pm\Zmax-z_v)\,\frac{\vec p_\perp}{p_z},
  \qquad
  \text{dropped if}\quad |\vec r_\perp(\pm\Zmax)|<R,
  \qquad \Zmax=R\sinh5.5 .
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:etacut}
\end{equation}
With $R=2.9\cm$, $\Zmax=2.9\times122.34=354.8\cm$; the number $5.5$ appears in
the configuration (\code{MinEtaCut}, \code{MaxEtaCut}) only to set $\Zmax$. For
a vertex on the beam line, $\vec r_{\perp,v}=0$, the test is
$(\Zmax\mp z_v)/\sinh|\eta|<R$ for a particle flying towards $\pm z$, which is an
effective cut
\begin{equation}
  \eta_c(z_v)=\sinh^{-1}\frac{\Zmax\mp z_v}{R}\simeq5.5\mp\frac{z_v}{\Zmax},
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:etac}
\end{equation}
since $\mathrm{d}\sinh^{-1}u/\mathrm{d}u\simeq1/u$ for large $u$: a vertex
$3.6\cm$ forward lowers the cut on its own side by $0.010$ and raises it on
the other. From the nominal point the line and the angle are the same test;
from any other vertex they are not. Nor is a vertex ever on the axis of the
cylinder: the beam spot of 2025 sits $r_v=0.33$\,mm from it (the $961$
vertices of our window are at $x=+285$, $y=-166\um$, each within $10\um$),
and the line of a particle of azimuth $\phi$ arrives at
\begin{equation}
  r(\pm\Zmax)\simeq\frac{\Zmax\mp z_v}{\sinh|\eta|}+r_v\cos(\phi-\phi_v),
  \qquad
  \eta_c\simeq5.5\mp\frac{z_v}{\Zmax}+\frac{r_v}{R}\cos(\phi-\phi_v),
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:etac_rv}
\end{equation}
so that the offset across the beam moves the cut by up to
$r_v/R=0.033/2.9=\pm0.011$, by the azimuth of the particle, as much as the
spread of the vertices along the beam does.

\subsection{The soft and the invisible: 40 MeV and the neutrinos}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:soft}

Two more rules turn members away, and both have a reason that is not
geometry. A particle of a few MeV cannot reach a sensitive volume, and its
energy is far below any threshold of the calorimeters; \code{Generator.cc}
drops every stable particle whose total momentum is below
$40\MeV$ (\code{MinPCut}) or, absurdly, above $99\,999\GeV$. And a neutrino
interacts with nothing: it passes every cut of \code{Generator.cc} and
becomes a primary, and the \code{StackingAction}, which looks at every track
before \GEANT starts it, classifies it \code{fKill} (\code{TrackNeutrino =
False}). Both are drawn in \cref{fig:crossing_the_border:soft}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\resizebox{0.95\textwidth}{!}{\input{chapter/tikz/g4_soft}}
\caption[The soft and the invisible]{The soft and the invisible. (a) A
$\pi^0$ of energy $E$ and velocity $\beta=p/E$ decays into two photons
emitted back to back at the angle $\theta^*$ to its flight in its rest
frame, with the laboratory energies $E_{1,2}=\frac{E}{2}(1\pm\beta\cos\theta^*)$,
uniform between $(E-p)/2$ and $(E+p)/2$ since $\cos\theta^*$ is; the shaded
part falls below the $40\MeV$ of \code{MinPCut}. Typical values: a $\pi^0$
of $E=1\GeV$ has $\beta=0.9909$; at $\cos\theta^*=0.50$ its photons carry
$E_1=748$ and $E_2=252\MeV$ and both are handed over; at
$\cos\theta^*=0.95$ they carry $971$ and $29\MeV$ and the second is
dropped, which happens whenever $|\cos\theta^*|>(1-2E_c/E)/\beta=0.928$,
in $7\,\%$ of the decays. (b) A neutrino passes the cuts of
\code{Generator.cc} and is killed by the \code{StackingAction} before its
first step; its transverse momentum is missing from the event from here on.}
\label{fig:crossing_the_border:soft}
\end{figure}

Who is soft? Almost only photons, and almost all of them from the decay of a
$\pi^0$. In its rest frame the two photons share $m_{\pi^0}$ equally and are
emitted isotropically, so that in the laboratory the energy of either is
uniform between the two extremes, and the chance that it falls below $E_c$ is
\begin{equation}
  P(E_\gamma<E_c)=\frac{E_c-(E-p)/2}{p}\simeq\frac{E_c}{E}
  \quad\text{for}\quad E\gg m_{\pi^0},
  \qquad
  E>\frac{E_c^2+m_{\pi^0}^2/4}{E_c}=154\MeV ,
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:pi0}
\end{equation}
the second condition being the energy below which no photon can be lost:
a slower $\pi^0$ gives both photons more than $40\MeV$ whatever the angle
(at rest, $67.5\MeV$ each). A $\pi^0$ of $1\GeV$ thus loses a photon to the cut
seven times in a hundred, one of $10\GeV$ less than once in a hundred. The
neutrinos carry what the detector will report as missing transverse
momentum, which at the generator level is simply
\begin{equation}
  \vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{miss}}=-\sum_{\text{visible}}\vec p_T=\sum_{\nu}\vec p_T ,
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:met}
\end{equation}
and is decided, particle by particle, at this gate.

For our event, from the \code{-v} log: five members of the family are below
$40\MeV$, four photons of $12.1$, $29.3$, $31.7$ and $39.5\MeV$ and an electron
of $4.1\MeV$, $0.12\GeV$ in all; the in-time crowd loses $143$, $3.6\GeV$. The
family's five neutrinos carry $229\GeV$, but two of them, of $53$ and
$51\GeV$, fly at $\eta=+5.2$ and $+5.3$ with half a GeV of transverse
momentum each, and the hardest, a $\bar\nu_\mu$ of $122\GeV$ at $\eta=-3.40$,
has $8.1\GeV$, near the $\bar b$ jet but not from its $B$ meson: the missing
transverse momentum of our event is $9.1\GeV$, and none of it comes from
the $b$ jet, whose $B$ meson decayed without a neutrino
(\cref{ch:children_producing_grandchildren}). The \CMSSW event is the opposite case: its $B^-$
decayed semileptonically, and the $\bar\nu_e$ of $95.5\GeV$ with $37.5\GeV$ of
transverse momentum leaves the $b$ system with $8$ of its $9$ particles and
$56.8$ of its $94.3\GeV$; the net transverse momentum of the two $b$ systems,
$20.3\GeV$ at hadron level, is $57.5\GeV$ among the primaries.
\Cref{fig:crossing_the_border:plot_soft} shows the momentum spectrum against
the cut, \cref{eq:crossing_the_border:pi0}, and where the energy that is not
handed over at the vertex goes.

\subsection{The decays the generator made: the cylinder of 2.9 cm}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:decays}

Some members of the family never reach the gate themselves, because the
generator has already decayed them. \PYTHIA, as \CMSSW configures it
(\code{ParticleDecays:limitTau0 = on}, \code{tau0Max = 10}\,mm), decays every
hadron whose mean decay length $c\tau$ is below $10$\,mm, the $B$ and $D$
mesons, the heavy baryons and the $\tau$, and leaves the longer-lived ones,
the $K^0_S$ ($26.8$\,mm), the $\Lambda$ ($78.9$\,mm), the charged pions and
kaons, to \GEANT, which knows where the material is
(\cref{ch:children_producing_grandchildren}). A decayed hadron stays in the record with status
$2$ and a decay vertex, and \code{Generator.cc} asks where that vertex is,
\cref{fig:crossing_the_border:decay}. Inside the cylinder $r<R$, the decay
happened in the vacuum of the beam pipe and is kept as the generator made it:
the daughters are primaries, starting at the decay point. Outside it the
parent itself is the primary, with the generator's decay attached as a
predefined channel and its proper time set to $\tau=L/(\beta\gamma c)$, so
that it crosses the material first and decays where the generator decided.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\resizebox{0.85\textwidth}{!}{\input{chapter/tikz/g4_decay}}
\caption[The decays the generator made, across the beam]{The decays the
generator made, seen across the beam: the cylinder $r<2.9\cm$ and the beam
pipe. Inside, the decay is kept and the daughters are primaries; outside,
the parent is the primary with its decay predefined; a long-lived hadron
such as the $K^0_S$ is not decayed by the generator at all.}
\label{fig:crossing_the_border:decay}
\end{figure}

Which of the two happens is a matter of chance. The flight length $L$ of a
hadron of mass $m$ and momentum $p$ is exponential with mean
$\beta\gamma c\tau=(p/m)c\tau$, and its distance from the beam at the decay,
$r=L\sin\theta$, is therefore exponential too, with mean
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_T=\frac{\pt}{m}\,c\tau,
  \qquad
  P(r_{\mathrm{dec}}>R)=e^{-R/\lambda_T}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{R\,m}{\pt\,c\tau}\right),
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:decay}
\end{equation}
which depends on the transverse momentum alone: a $B$ meson flying along the
beam travels far without moving away from it. For our family, from the
\code{-v} log: \PYTHIA made $376$ decays, $14$ of them weak, and every one of
them inside the cylinder. The $\bar B^0$ of the $b$ jet ($\pt=78.3\GeV$,
$\beta\gamma=31.2$) flew $2.10\cm$ and decayed $0.97\cm$ from the beam, where
$\lambda_T=0.68\cm$ gives a chance of $1.4\,\%$ to reach $2.9\cm$; the
$\bar B^0$ of the $\bar b$ jet, a $B^0$ that oscillated
(\cref{ch:children_producing_grandchildren}), with $\beta\gamma=246$, flew $14.7\cm$ along the
beam and decayed $0.97\cm$ from it, a $1.9\,\%$ chance; the farthest from the
beam, a $D^0$ of $\pt=27\GeV$ from the $b$ side, decayed at $r=1.48\cm$. So every
daughter of our event starts as a primary where its parent decayed, and no
predefined decay is needed; the \CMSSW event says the same of its $28$ decay
vertices, the farthest a $\bar D^0$ $25.8\cm$ from the collision but $1.2\cm$
from the beam. \Cref{fig:crossing_the_border:plot_decays} draws
\cref{eq:crossing_the_border:decay} for the hadrons the generator decays and,
dashed, for the $K^0_S$ and the $\Lambda$ as if it did: a $K^0_S$ of
$1\GeV$ would decay outside the cylinder $58\,\%$ of the time, which is why
\code{tau0Max} leaves it to \GEANT.

\subsection{The forward: the Zero Degree Calorimeter and the Roman pots}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:forward}

The last rules are for the particles that leave the collision almost along
the beam, and they exist because CMS has two detectors far from its centre,
\cref{fig:crossing_the_border:forward}. Between the beam pipes, $140$\,m away
on each side, the Zero Degree Calorimeter~\cite{Grachov:2006ke} measures the
neutral particles that fly straight on, neutrons above all, while the
charged ones are bent away by the magnets of the LHC. And $203$\,m away, the
Roman pots of the Precision Proton Spectrometer~\cite{CMS:2014sdw,CMS:2022dmc}
approach the beam to a few millimetres to catch the protons that survived
the collision with a small loss of momentum. Both are fed by the hand-over.
They are described here for completeness, because the gate has a rule for
each: our signal, its two jets and everything the later stops will measure
of them, lies within $|\eta|<5.5$, and from the next stop on the journey
follows only the primaries handed over there, into the tracker, the
calorimeters and the muon chambers. The Zero Degree Calorimeter and the
Roman pots will not appear again.
\code{Generator.cc} follows the line of every stable particle to
$|z|=\Zhec=R\sinh8.2=52.8$\,m (\code{EtaCutForHector}) and, if it is still
inside the cylinder there, hands it to \GEANT only if it is a neutron, an
antineutron or a photon; the other very forward particles are left to the
PPS transport, which takes every proton with
\begin{equation}
  |\eta|>8.2,\qquad |p_z|>3\TeV
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \xi=1-\frac{|\vec p|}{E_{\mathrm{beam}}}\lesssim0.56 ,
  \label{eq:crossing_the_border:pps}
\end{equation}
carries it through the optics of the LHC magnets and hands it to \GEANT at
the Roman pots (the Run-3 setup of the release switches its empirical
apertures off). Everything else beyond $|\eta|\simeq5.5$ is dropped. Whether
the neutrals reach the ZDC is another matter: with the default stepping of
the release (\code{CMStoZDCtransport = False}) they are killed when they
enter the beam-line volume \code{CMStoZDC}, a dead region between
$|z|=27.5$ and $139$\,m, a metre in front of the calorimeter; the
very-forward rule hands them over so that a study of the ZDC can switch
the transport on.

\section{The energy ledger: our jet at this stage}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:ledger}

\label{sec:crossing_the_border:record_format}
The record, \file{example/G4/event_G4.lhe}, keeps every row of the record of
\cref{ch:crowd_on_the_road} and adds four tables, which the companion prints
with a guide to their columns: the \emph{summary} of the gate and its counts;
the \emph{hand-over listing}, one row for every stable particle of the
family and of the in-time crowd with its vertex, the radius of its line at
$\Zmax$, the decision of the gate (primary, very-forward primary, neutrino
killed, eta cut, very forward but not a neutral, momentum cut, Roman pots)
and the reason the ledger books it under; the \emph{collision listing}, one
row for each of the $961$ collisions of the window, the signal first, with
its particles before and its primaries after; and the \emph{decay listing},
the $376$ decays the generator made in the family, with their decay vertices.

Our jet crosses the gate whole. Its $31$ particles are all stable hadrons
and photons well above $40\MeV$ within $|\eta|<2.5$, and the decays inside
it all happened within $1.5\cm$ of the beam, so that every member becomes a
primary and the ledger of \cref{tab:crossing_the_border:ledger} has the

\subsection{What this stop is, and what it is not}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:caveats}

The agreement with TGeo and \PYTHIA in \cref{tab:crossing_the_border:numbers}
is exact where the input is identical, and the parts of the chapter that
are a model rather than a port carry their caveats:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A port of the rules, not of \GEANT.} The script decides what
    \code{Generator.cc}, the \code{StackingAction} and the PPS transport
    decide; it does not move a single particle through the detector. The
    field, the material, the showers and the hits are
    \cref{ch:crowd_at_the_border}, and the counts that turn them into
    signals \cref{ch:customs_form}.
  \item \textbf{Straight lines from vertices.} The eta cut, the
    very-forward rule and the decay cylinder are tests on straight lines and
    points; they ignore the field, as \code{Generator.cc} does, so that a
    soft charged particle that would curl back into the beam pipe is handed
    over if its line leaves the cylinder. The lines start where the vertices
    are: $0.33$\,mm from the axis of the cylinder in 2025, which moves the
    cut by up to $0.011$ with the azimuth
    (\cref{eq:crossing_the_border:etac_rv}).
  \item \textbf{The crowd has no displaced vertices.} The library of
    \cref{ch:crowd_on_the_road} stores the final particles of each collision
    at its vertex, so that a pileup particle from a heavy-flavour decay starts
    at the collision rather than a millimetre away; the effect on the line is
    below a micron in $r$ for most of them, and six particles whose $p_z$ the
    library rounded to zero are dropped by the literal port.
  \item \textbf{The PPS transport is a selection here.} The script picks the
    protons the PPS transport takes and books them at the Roman pots; it does
    not propagate them through the optical functions, which change their
    direction and position, not whether they arrive (the Run-3 setup of the
    release has no aperture model).
  \item \textbf{The family alone decides nothing about the crowd.} The gate
    is applied collision by collision, as \CMSSW applies it to the library,
    and our in-time and out-of-time crowds are one draw of \cref{ch:crowd_on_the_road}.
  \item \textbf{One event is one sample.} Our jet crossing the gate whole, the
    $9.1\GeV$ of missing \pt, the $27$ protons at the Roman pots and the
    $114$ and $93$ particles decided by the line are this event's.
\end{itemize}

\section{Further reading}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:further_reading}
The toolkit is \GEANT, described in \cite{GEANT4:2002zbu} and in its two
later reviews~\cite{Allison:2006ve,Allison:2016lfl}; the CMS simulation built
on it, its geometry, its sensitive detectors and its performance, in
\cite{Hildreth:2017caz,Pedro:2019CMSsim}; the detector itself in
\cite{CMS:2008xjf} and its Run-3 state in \cite{CMS:2024aqx}. The passage of
particles through matter, which the next stop begins, is the review of the
Particle Data Group~\cite{ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk}, and calorimetry the
book of Wigmans~\cite{Wigmans:2000vf}. The forward detectors of this stop are
the Zero Degree Calorimeter~\cite{Grachov:2006ke} and the Precision Proton
Spectrometer, from its technical design report~\cite{CMS:2014sdw} to the
reconstruction of its protons in Run~2~\cite{CMS:2022dmc}; the decays the
generator makes are those of \PYTHIA~8.3~\cite{Bierlich:2022pfr}, and the
geometry package of the validation is TGeo~\cite{Brun:2003TGeo}.

\begin{pitfalls}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{``\GEANT simulates every particle of the event.''} It
    simulates the primaries, and the hand-over drops half of the family's
    energy and three quarters of the crowd's before the first step; a
    generator-level sum of energy is not what the simulation starts from.
  \item \emph{``The eta cut is $|\eta|<5.5$.''} It is a line from the vertex
    to $|z|=354.8\cm$; from a vertex $3.6\cm$ away the effective cut moves by
    $0.010$, and a particle at $\eta=5.505$ can be kept while one at $5.4905$
    is dropped.
  \item \emph{``The event loses $4.5\,\%$ of its \pt at the gate, so our
    jet does too.''} The $4.5\,\%$ is of the scalar \pt of all $749$
    particles and is lost beyond $|\eta|=5.5$ and with the neutrinos; the
    $31$ constituents of our jet are all handed over. A loss booked for a
    collision is not a loss of a jet: ask where it happens.
  \item \emph{``Particles between $|\eta|=5.2$ and $5.5$ will be seen by HF.''}
    They are handed over, but from the centre a line beyond $5.32$ passes
    through the hole of HF entirely: most of them leave no hit.
  \item \emph{``Neutrinos are dropped by the generator interface.''} They pass
    \code{Generator.cc} and become primaries; the \code{StackingAction} kills
    them. A configuration with \code{TrackNeutrino = True} (the CPU benchmark
    of the release) keeps them, and pays for it.
  \item \emph{``The $B$ meson is a \GEANT primary.''} Only if it decays
    beyond $2.9\cm$ from the beam; otherwise its daughters are, and the
    \code{SimTrack} of a $B$ meson is rare. The truth link from a
    \code{SimTrack} to the generator is \code{genpartIndex()}, the barcode of
    the daughter.
  \item \emph{``The forward protons are lost.''} Those with $|p_z|>3\TeV$
    beyond $|\eta|=8.2$ reach \GEANT at the Roman pots, $203$\,m away, and
    their \code{SimTrack}s point into the record of the transport, not into
    the generator record.
  \item \emph{``The hand-over depends on the year.''} The 2024 and 2025
    settings of the release give the same primaries, vertex for vertex: the
    year starts to matter with the calibrations of the digitisation.
\end{itemize}
\end{pitfalls}

\subsection*{Open problems in the context of Phase-2 LHC}
\label{sec:crossing_the_border:open_problems}
The hand-over is configured once for all eras in \file{g4SimHits_cfi.py},
and the HL-LHC detector of the Phase-2 upgrade~\cite{CMS:2015zni} inherits
it almost unchanged; the companion compared the module of the release under
the default Phase-2 era with the one of 2025 and found the same \code{Generator}
and \code{StackingAction} parameters. Where that matters, and where it might
not be enough, is a list of questions a student can study with the scripts of
this stop.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The eta cut and the hole of HF.} The Phase-2 detector keeps
    the forward calorimeter of Run~3, built from the same constants, so that
    the hole still starts at $|\eta|=5.18$ and the cut stays at $5.5$, while
    the Phase-2 Level-1 trigger~\cite{CMS:2020jkc} defines its forward
    regions out to $5.5$. Is a cut at $5.2$ (exercise~2) safe for the
    calorimeters and worth the time it saves, with two hundred collisions
    per crossing?
  \item \textbf{The decay cylinder and the new inner tracker.} The
    Phase-2 inner tracker~\cite{CMS:2017lum} puts the rods of its first layer
    at $r=2.75$ and $3.25\cm$, so that the cylinder of $2.9\cm$ ends between
    them: a generator decay at $2.75<r<2.9\cm$ is kept as the generator made it
    although its parent may have crossed silicon. How often does this happen
    for $B$ hadrons and for the long-lived particles of new-physics searches,
    and should \code{RDecLenCut} follow the beam pipe of the new geometry?
  \item \textbf{The protons and the Roman pots.} The Phase-2 era switches
    the PPS transport off, so that the forward protons, which the very-forward
    rule does not hand over, would not enter \GEANT at all; the proton
    spectrometer of the HL-LHC is a proposal of its own~\cite{CMS:2021ncv}.
    What would its acceptance be for the diffractive collisions of a
    $\mu=200$ crowd, and how many protons per crossing would compete with
    the signal's?
    \item \textbf{The very forward neutrals and the ZDC.} The very-forward
    rule hands the neutrons and photons beyond $|\eta|\simeq8.2$ to \GEANT,
    $124\TeV$ of them per crossing at $\mu=60$ and three times more at
    $200$, and in 2025 the default stepping kills them in the dead region of
    the beam line before the ZDC. The Phase-2 stepping of the release, chosen
    as soon as the geometry has the new endcap calorimeter or the timing
    layer, does not read the flag that would let them through, so that the
    ZDC of the HL-LHC geometry is simulated and receives none of them, while
    the beam-line instrumentation around it is being
    upgraded~\cite{CMS:2021BRIL}. Should these primaries be handed over at
    all in the samples that do not study the forward region, and how would
    a study that does reach the ZDC?
  \item \textbf{The cost of the crowd.} \GEANT receives about $140$
    primaries per collision and $25\,\%$ of its energy, so that a crossing of
    the HL-LHC hands it some $30\,000$ primaries, most of them soft and
    forward, into the high-granularity endcap
    calorimeter~\cite{CMS:2017jpq} and the timing layer~\cite{Butler:2019rpu}.
    Premixing amortises the cost over many signals~\cite{Hildreth:2017caz};
    transport on graphics processors~\cite{Johnson:2021Celeritas,Amadio:2023AdePT}
    and faster physics in the forward region may decide whether a
    Phase-2 sample can be simulated at all. Which of the primaries of this
    chapter dominate the cost, and could the hand-over itself drop more of
    them without changing what the detector sees?
\end{enumerate}
The next stop lets every primary of the window cross the border, the
family and the crowd together, through the field, the material and the
time windows of the detector.
